What the audit is for

A 100-point check of the whole business, done at every weekly Area Manager visit. It covers sales, margin, stock, service, staff, compliance, systems and running costs.

Accountability: every store manager knows exactly what is expected each week. Performance: see why sales move, where money is lost and what to fix. Improvement: every visit ends with actions, owners, deadlines and a follow-up check.

The weekly cycle

  • At the visit: score the Audit and Operations tabs. Add notes and photos as evidence wherever something is wrong.
  • Before you leave: open Actions & tasks, mark last visit's tasks done or not done, and give each new task an owner. Copy the list to the team.
  • Finish visit: on the Summary tab. This saves the week and starts next week's checklist with the open tasks carried over.
  • For management: the Management report tab shows scores, issues found and fixed, task completion and purchases against budget, week by week.

The Operations score is shown separately (out of 100) so the 100-point audit stays comparable with earlier visits.

Follow-up deadlines

Repeated failureEscalate to senior managementNext review

Repeat-failure rule: the same issue in three weekly inspections in a row becomes a management performance concern, even if the store scores well overall.

The 90-minute visit

0–15 minWalk the store as a customer: presentation, cleanliness, availability, service
15–40 minPhysical checks: shelves, stockroom, fridges, expiry dates, safety, security
40–60 minNumbers: sales, gross profit, cash, commission, wastage, labour
60–75 minTalk to the manager and staff: problems, customer feedback, ideas
75–90 minAgree the score, assign actions and deadlines, review last week's actions

The store trades 24 hours, so rotate visit times: daytime some weeks, evenings in others, and overnight now and then.
